Identifier architecture

DPP identifiers: connect the physical product to the right digital record.

ESPR requires a persistent unique product identifier linked through a data carrier. Product-specific rules determine the required model, batch or item level.

Keep these concepts separate

Product identifier

Persistent identifier for the relevant model, batch or item.

Operator identifier

Identifies the economic operator where required.

Facility identifier

Identifies relevant production or value-chain locations.

Registry identifier

Supports EU-level registration while passport data remains decentralised.

Architecture rule: keep the identifier stable even if the user-facing page or service provider changes.

Why identifier governance matters

The identifier is the anchor that connects product data, the physical data carrier, Registry metadata and downstream systems. If an identifier changes unnecessarily, links between those layers can break even when the underlying product has not changed.

Model, batch or item level

Different product rules can require different passport granularity. A model-level identifier may be sufficient for one product group, while another may require batch-level or individual-item identification. Do not mix those levels in one field without clear governance.

Identifier design checklist

  • Stable over the required product lifecycle
  • Machine-readable and easy to exchange
  • Clear ownership and namespace
  • Separate from marketing names and descriptions
  • Usable in URLs, APIs and physical data carriers
  • Compatible with the chosen sector or identification standard

GTIN and GS1

GTIN can be relevant where GS1 identifiers are used, and GS1 Digital Link can make those identifiers web-resolvable. Neither should be assumed to be mandatory for every DPP sector unless the applicable rules require them.
