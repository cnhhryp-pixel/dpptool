Data carrier guide

A DPP QR code is a doorway, not the passport itself.

Under ESPR, the data carrier connects a persistent unique product identifier to Digital Product Passport data. The exact carrier and placement come from the applicable product-specific rules.

Three layers to separate

1. Identifier

A persistent unique product identifier identifies the relevant model, batch or item.

2. Data carrier

A QR code, barcode or other automatic identification medium makes the link machine-readable.

3. Passport data

The underlying structured data contains the information required for the product group and access level.

Can I just put a QR code on my product?

No. A generic QR code linking to a marketing page is not automatically a compliant DPP. Product-specific rules determine required information, identifier level, data carrier, placement, access rights and lifetime.

Official source

  • ESPR Articles 9–12 and Annex III — EUR-Lex

How a DPP QR workflow is usually designed

  1. Create or assign the persistent product identifier.
  2. Build a stable resolver or passport URL.
  3. Encode that URL or identifier into the permitted data carrier.
  4. Connect the resolver to the correct passport data and access rules.
  5. Test scanning, redirects and long-term hosting before mass printing.

QR code vs passport data

LayerPurpose
IdentifierIdentifies the product, model, batch or item.
QR / data carrierMakes the identifier or resolver machine-readable.
Resolver / URLConnects the scan to the appropriate digital resource.
DPP dataContains the structured information required by the applicable rules.

Common implementation mistake

Printing a QR code that points to a static marketing page can look like a “digital passport” but does not by itself satisfy DPP requirements. The underlying identifier, structured data, access rights and lifecycle availability still need to be designed correctly.
