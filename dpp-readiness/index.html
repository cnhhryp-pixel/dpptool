What this score measures

DPP readiness is mostly a data-governance question: identifiers, product master quality, supplier evidence, compliance documents, lifecycle information, ownership and exportability. A high score does not by itself mean the product is legally compliant.

Use the scope checker too: readiness answers “can we produce reliable passport data?” while the DPP Checker answers “what is the current regulatory position for this product group?”

How the readiness score is calculated

Each of the 10 questions is scored as 0 for “No / not yet”, 1 for “Partly” and 2 for “Yes”. The tool converts the total into a percentage and highlights the data-governance gaps that are likely to slow implementation.

What good DPP readiness looks like

Stable identifiers

Products, suppliers and documents can be referenced consistently across systems.

Reliable evidence

Supplier and compliance records are linked to the right product and version.

Portable data

Core product data can be exported in structured formats rather than manually rebuilt.

Frequently asked questions

What does the DPP Readiness score measure?

It measures operational data readiness across identifiers, product master quality, supplier evidence, compliance records, field ownership, exportability, versioning and lifecycle information.

Does a high readiness score mean my product is compliant?

No. A high score means your data foundation is stronger. Legal compliance still depends on the applicable product-specific rules.

What should I fix first if my score is low?

Start with stable product identifiers, a clean product master, supplier evidence and compliance documentation because those foundations support most later DPP work.
